In a company car something else travels alongside the driver: the employment relationship. A camera recording there therefore touches not only road traffic but the question of who is observing whom.

> [!WARNING]
> This article gives general orientation and is not legal advice. Settle the details with the responsible people in your organisation.

## Who decides about the camera

That depends on who owns the vehicle and who operates the camera. A vehicle owned by the employer is company equipment, and a permanently fitted camera is a decision for the organisation rather than for the individual driver.

Conversely, a private camera in a company car is not a given either. It records journeys made on company business and may capture colleagues.

## The employee perspective

A camera in a vehicle creates a record of when someone travelled where and how they drove. From the point of view of employee data protection that is a considerable intrusion, even when it is not intended.

A workplace agreement is therefore the right route. Where staff representation exists, it is normally involved in matters like this. A clear agreement removes the uncertainty for everyone.

## Purpose limitation is decisive

A camera for documenting collisions must not become a performance monitor. As soon as footage is used to assess driving behaviour or working hours, the character of the recording changes fundamentally.

So set down what footage is used for, who has access and how long it is kept. Without those three points any arrangement is incomplete.

## Interior recording is its own case

A camera filming the passenger compartment is intended for passenger services where what happens inside matters. In an ordinary company car it is hard to justify.

The same applies to audio. Conversations enjoy particular protection, and business calls happen in company cars. The article on [audio in your clip](/en/blog/audio-in-your-clip) explains why the microphone stays off when in doubt.

## If you want to submit footage

Footage from a company car is not automatically yours. Before submitting to a channel, check whether your employer agrees, particularly if the vehicle carries visible branding.

Company lettering in frame makes the organisation identifiable, and that is not a decision for you alone.

## What you can do as a driver

Ask before using your own camera. A short question to fleet management or the responsible department settles it, and you know where you stand before it matters.

If a camera is already fitted, find out what is recorded, who can access it and how long the data is kept. You are entitled to those details, and they should be available in writing.

## For organisations

A clear written arrangement is easier to produce than to explain after the fact. It names the purpose, the retention period, who may access the footage and the circumstances in which recordings are reviewed.

Technically a short recording loop belongs to it, as described in the article on [loop recording](/en/blog/understanding-loop-recording). Anything stored permanently beyond that needs a reason of its own.

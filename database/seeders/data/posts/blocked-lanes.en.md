A blocked emergency lane, an obstructed cycle path, a vehicle on the pavement outside a nursery: such situations are quickly filmed, and the question of what to do with the footage is less clear cut than the irritation of the moment.

## The emergency lane is a special case

This is not about everyday infringements but about access for emergency services. Footage can show how traffic behaved as an emergency vehicle approached, and it can establish whether a lane was formed or not.

Your own role matters: attention belongs to forming the lane first, not to the camera. The recording happens by itself anyway, because the camera is running.

## Everyday illegal parking

Restraint is appropriate here. A report is possible in principle, and in cases of genuine obstruction, such as on footways and cycle paths, in disabled parking bays or in front of fire service access, it is understandable.

Anyone systematically photographing and reporting vehicles, however, quickly leaves the territory of being personally affected. That point is delicate under data protection law too, because recordings then no longer arise from a specific cause.

> This article gives general orientation and is not legal advice.

## What footage should show

If you document a situation, make sure the obstruction is recognisable. A vehicle on a cycle path is, without the cycle path in frame, simply a parked car.

Date and time help, as does the duration if you know it, and enough surroundings to establish the location. Keep to the vehicle concerned and avoid capturing passers-by more clearly than necessary.

## If you want to submit it

Such footage interests channels when it shows a pattern rather than exposing one individual. A blocked emergency lane on a motorway concerns everyone; a neighbour car on the pavement concerns the neighbour.

Blur plates where they contribute nothing to the scene. How to do that reliably is described in the article on [blurring faces and plates](/en/blog/blurring-faces-and-plates).

## The simple test

Ask yourself whether the footage explains a situation or exposes a person. In the first case it is useful; in the second you should keep it or delete it. The article on [publishing footage](/en/blog/publishing-footage) works through that balance in more detail.

## Do not fiddle while driving

One rule covers all these situations: the camera is running anyway. There is no reason to reach for a phone while driving or to change position for a better shot.

If you want to keep a scene, press the button that protects the current recording at most, and only once the traffic situation allows.

## What not to do

Confronting other road users, commenting on the incident while driving on, or blocking a vehicle to get a better shot. A documented infringement otherwise turns quickly into your own contribution to the hazard.

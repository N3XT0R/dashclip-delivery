A number of claims about dashcams circulate persistently. Six of them come up again and again, and none holds in the form it is told.

> [!WARNING]
> This article gives general orientation and is not legal advice.

## Dashcams are banned in Germany

Wrong. Neither owning nor operating a camera is prohibited as such. What causes problems is recording permanently without cause, meaning storing everything the camera sees.

Running a short recording loop and securing footage only when there is a concrete reason puts you in a considerably different position. The article on [dashcams and data protection](/en/blog/dashcams-and-data-protection) sets it out.

## Footage is never accepted in court

Also wrong. The German Federal Court of Justice held that footage can be used as evidence in accident liability proceedings, even where its collection was objectionable under data protection law. What decides it is a balancing exercise in the individual case.

## Whoever has a dashcam always wins

Wrong too, and in both directions. Footage shows what happened, including your own mistakes. It also shows only a section, and that section is distorted by field of view and light, as the article on [the limits of any recording](/en/blog/what-the-camera-does-not-see) describes.

## Higher resolution solves everything

No. Without sufficient bitrate even high resolution footage breaks into blocks during movement, and that is exactly when a plate becomes unreadable. The article on [resolution, bitrate and frame rate](/en/blog/resolution-bitrate-frame-rate) explains the relationship.

## The camera is running, so it must be recording

That is the most expensive assumption of all. Memory cards fail silently, cameras freeze, and settings revert after an update. Without regular checks you do not know whether a file is being created.

## German law applies abroad

No, the law of the country you are driving in applies. In Austria, Switzerland, Luxembourg and Portugal operating a camera is heavily restricted or prohibited, regardless of where your vehicle is registered. The article on [using a dashcam abroad](/en/blog/dashcams-abroad) collects the details.

## What remains

A dashcam is a useful tool with clear limits. Run it sparingly, check it regularly, secure the file when it matters and think before publishing. There is not much more to it.

## Two more that nearly made the list

A camera in the vehicle acts as a deterrent. It does not: nobody can tell in passing whether a device behind the glass is recording or merely mounted for show.

Footage may be published freely because it was recorded in public. That is wrong too. Public space does not mean the people shown there have no rights, as the article on [publishing footage](/en/blog/publishing-footage) describes.

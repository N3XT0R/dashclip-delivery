Most footage worth having shows no collision. It shows the moment before, when everything worked out after all. That is exactly what makes it interesting, and exactly why it is secured too rarely.

## Why a near miss shows more than a crash

In a collision the focus lands on the impact. In a near miss attention stays on what prevented it: braking in time, a swerve, a driver who was paying attention.

For road safety that is the more valuable information. Anyone who sees how a situation develops and how it can be defused recognises it earlier next time.

## The sensor will not help you here

An impact sensor triggers only when something strikes something. In a near miss that is precisely what does not happen, and the recording stays unprotected. The loop overwrites it like any other.

The manual protection button is therefore the only rescue in these situations. Anyone who cannot find it without looking loses the footage. The article on [setting the impact sensor](/en/blog/setting-the-impact-sensor) describes how to set both up.

> [!NOTE]
> Press the button only when the situation allows. The recording stays available for several minutes, and your attention belongs to the road first.

## What makes good near miss footage

The lead-in matters even more here than usual. The interesting part is how the situation developed, not the absence of damage. Fifteen seconds beforehand beats ten.

The description should say what you did. Whether you braked, swerved or simply lifted off early is exactly the information that makes the clip instructive.

## Particularly suited to channels

Channels working on road safety often use such material, because it needs no schadenfreude and exposes nobody. The legal position is easier too: there are no proceedings under way that a publication could affect.

The usual still applies: consider bystanders in frame and blur plates where they contribute nothing to the scene. The article on [plates and faces](/en/blog/plates-and-faces-before-you-submit) collects it.

## Useful to you as well

Watch such footage properly rather than only submitting it. It often shows something that went unnoticed at the time: that the gap was smaller than it felt, that a reaction came later than you remember, that visibility was worse than you assumed.

That is uncomfortable and instructive at once, and it is the real everyday value of a camera.

## When footage is more than instructive

Some near misses are also a serious breach: overtaking before a blind bend, running a red light with pedestrians in the way, a vehicle following closely again and again. That raises the question of whether a report is appropriate.

A simple guide: the more concrete the danger was, the more the route to the police is worth taking. What to consider is described in the article on [presenting footage to the police](/en/blog/presenting-footage-to-the-police).

## Collecting them is not worthwhile

Resist the temptation to build an archive of every near miss. Keeping footage permanently without cause is neither necessary nor justifiable. Secure what you actually intend to use and let the rest be overwritten.

This article closes a year. It began with a simple observation: there is a lot to explain about dashcams and submitting clips, and much of it sits scattered or nowhere at all.

## What has come together

Three areas have proven durable. The news articles explain how the platform works, from the first upload through channel selection to the states in your overview.

The submission tips cover the craft: choosing the right section, writing the description, handling bystanders in frame and the question of how many channels make sense.

Dashcam knowledge has grown into the largest area, from the purchase decision through mounting and settings to the legal questions that count when something happens.

## What turned out to matter most

Laying the articles side by side, three points repeat, and not by accident.

First: a camera helps only if it is actually recording. Silent failure is the most common reason footage is missing, and a short check once a month prevents it.

Second: footage has to be secured before the loop overwrites it. Everything else comes afterwards.

Third: working sparingly with data is not a restriction but the precondition for footage being worth anything when it counts.

## What stayed open

Some questions can only be answered in general terms. The legal position turns on the individual case, and rules abroad change. The articles concerned therefore point to their sources rather than offering a seemingly final answer.

Technically things keep moving too: cameras improve, memory cards get faster, and expectations of what footage has to show rise with them.

## Where things go from here

The subjects covered here come from what people actually need. If you meet a question that is not answered here, that is the best pointer to the next article.

Until then, what was true at the start still holds: the [article search](/en/blog/search) finds pieces by title, summary and text, the categories narrow things down, and the [feed](/en/blog/feed.xml) announces new articles. Anyone who has just arrived is best served by starting with [your first upload](/en/blog/your-first-upload).

## A note about the blog itself

The articles here deliberately point to one another rather than explaining every subject repeatedly. Anyone landing on mounting finds alignment from there, then plate legibility, then the settings for resolution and bitrate.

That makes the blog a reference work rather than a collection of separate texts. It rewards reading through once at leisure and coming back for specifics later.

## For channel operators

Part of the material addresses the other side explicitly: channels that receive and manage submissions. If you run a channel, the articles on deadlines, responses and handling people in frame are worth your time, because they describe what submitters expect of you.

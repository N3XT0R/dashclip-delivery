A camera on a motorcycle has the same job as one in a car and works under entirely different conditions. No cabin, no windscreen, constant vibration and weather from every direction. That shifts the requirements considerably.

## Weather sealing is not an optional extra

In a car the bodywork protects the camera; on a motorcycle nothing does. The camera needs a waterproof housing or a design built for it. Splash resistance is not enough, because rain at motorway speed hits the housing with force.

Pay equal attention to the connectors. They are where moisture enters first, and a camera that fails in the rain is useless exactly when the road is at its most dangerous.

## Vibration destroys more than falls

A motorcycle transmits vibration directly to anything fixed to the frame or the handlebars. That causes two problems: the footage becomes unsteady, and memory cards and contacts age faster.

A decoupled mount helps with both. It costs a little more and stops usable footage turning into a shaking sequence of frames.

> [!NOTE]
> Check the mount after every longer ride. What works loose on a car over months works loose on a motorcycle in days.

## Power and riding time

Motorcycles offer less reserve than cars, and many rides are short. A permanent connection to the electrical system should therefore include protection that cuts out at low voltage.

Expect as well that the camera boots afresh at every start. Devices that take a long time over it miss the first few minutes of every ride.

## Perspective and field of view

Mounted on the handlebars the camera swings with every steering input, which makes cornering footage restless. A mount on the frame or the fairing gives a steadier image but covers a narrower area.

Mounted on the helmet the camera follows your gaze, which helps someone follow the sequence. In that case check in advance whether attachments to the helmet are acceptable, because views on that differ.

## What applies to the footage

Legally nothing differs from a car. Short recording loop, microphone off, publish nothing without checking. The basics are in the article on [dashcams and data protection](/en/blog/dashcams-and-data-protection), and for rides across a border the article on [using a dashcam abroad](/en/blog/dashcams-abroad) applies as well.

## Footage from a motorcycle is often especially clear

Because the riding position is high and the view unobstructed, motorcycle footage often shows situations more clearly than car footage. Being overlooked is the classic case, and that sequence is usually unambiguous on a recording.

That makes such clips interesting for channels working on road safety. When submitting, be generous with the lead-in: on two wheels the approach is the decisive phase.

## After the ride

Take the camera off when the bike stands for a longer period. On a motorcycle it is far easier to steal than inside a locked car, and the mount does not survive constant sun and rain indefinitely anyway.

After a few months a clip archive usually looks like this: one folder with forty files named the way the camera named them. Which of them was the near miss in November, nobody knows any more. A simple scheme prevents that.

## Why camera names are not enough

File names such as 20261114_073212 contain the date and time but nothing about what is visible. To search, you have to open every file, and nobody does that.

On top of that, many cameras assign names with sequential numbers that repeat after a format. Two different recordings then sit side by side under the same name.

## A scheme that works

Date, place, keyword. For example: 2026-11-14-b51-deer-crossing. The date in that form sorts chronologically by itself, the place narrows things down, and the keyword says what it was about.

Where several files belong to one event, append the perspective: one marked front, one marked rear. Recordings that belong together then stay together.

## Folders by month

One folder per month is entirely enough. Finer structures sound tidy and stop being maintained after a fortnight.

Keep secured recordings separate from material awaiting review: one folder for what you intend to keep and one for what you are still checking. Whatever has been checked and discarded gets deleted.

> [!NOTE]
> Keep originals and edited versions strictly apart. An anonymised file does not belong where you look for the original when an insurer asks for it.

## One line of notes per recording

This is the part most people skip and the one that pays off most. A text file per month with one line per recording is enough: date, time, place, what happened, whether you submitted it and to whom.

In a fortnight you will not need it. In six months it is the only source still accurate.

## What to delete again

Everything that no longer has a reason to exist. A secured recording whose claim has been settled is needed by nobody. An archive of uneventful drives never had a reason.

Set a fixed moment for it, such as the same day you do your [monthly quick check](/en/blog/your-monthly-dashcam-check). The article on [archiving footage](/en/blog/archiving-footage) describes how long keeping something can be justified.

## Backups belong to it

A single copy is not a backup. Store secured recordings on external storage as well, and check once a year that the files there still open.

External drives and memory cards fail silently. Anyone who notices only when they need a file has already lost it.

## The effort in numbers

A file name takes ten seconds, a line of notes twenty. At one secured recording a month, that is six minutes a year.

Against that stands the case where you need a particular recording and cannot find it. The arithmetic is not close.

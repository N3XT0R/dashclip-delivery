Power supply decides more than whether the camera comes on. It determines whether parking mode works, how tidy the cabin looks and whether your vehicle battery still has reserve in the morning.

## The accessory socket route

The simplest route is an adapter in the accessory socket. No intervention in the vehicle, done in five minutes, and on most cars the socket switches off with the ignition so the camera only runs while driving.

The drawbacks are equally obvious. The cable hangs across the cabin, the socket is occupied, and parking mode is impossible once the socket loses power. For getting started this route is still right, because it commits to nothing.

## Hardwiring

With hardwiring the camera is connected to the fuse box, usually through an adapter that taps two circuits: one permanently live for parking mode and one switched, from which the camera detects whether the ignition is on.

Low voltage protection is essential. It switches the camera off once battery voltage falls below a set value. Without it, a camera running permanently drains the battery to the point where the vehicle will not start.

> [!WARNING]
> On modern vehicles with battery management or stop-start systems, hardwiring should be done by a qualified workshop. Choosing the wrong circuit can trigger fault messages or malfunctions.

## An auxiliary battery

A separate battery powers the camera independently of the vehicle and charges while you drive. It is the most involved option but also the gentlest: the vehicle battery stays untouched, and parking mode runs for many hours depending on capacity.

Consider weight, space and price. Such batteries face the same temperature limits as everything else in a vehicle, and in high summer they age accordingly faster.

## Routing cables cleanly

Whichever route you take, the cable belongs behind the trim rather than across the dashboard. Run it along the headliner to the A-pillar and from there into the footwell. Keep clear of airbags; many vehicles have one inside the A-pillar.

Use a cable long enough to leave a small loop in reserve. A cable pulled tight slowly drags the camera out of its mount as temperatures change.

## What to check after fitting

Does the camera start reliably with the ignition? Does it switch off after you park, or move into parking mode? Is a file actually created in both states?

Those three questions tell you whether the power supply does its job. The article on [parking mode](/en/blog/parking-mode) covers the settings that additionally matter while parked.

Traffic footage almost always shows people and vehicles that have nothing to do with the scene itself. A pedestrian at a crossing, a plate in oncoming traffic, someone waiting at a bus stop. It is worth looking at that deliberately before submitting.

## Why this concerns you

While the recording sits on your memory card and is overwritten there, the circle of people affected is small. The moment you submit it, it leaves your control. The channel then decides about publication, and published material is practically impossible to retrieve.

That does not mean every recording has to be anonymised. It means you should know what is visible in your clip before you pass it on.

## The difference between involved and uninvolved

People involved in a scene are part of the events you want to show. Their vehicle, their behaviour and the sequence are the content of the recording.

Uninvolved people are in frame by chance. They contribute nothing to understanding it, and their being identifiable has no value for the scene. This is exactly where the question of anonymising is worth asking.

> [!NOTE]
> A number plate is not a minor detail. It can be traced to a person, which is precisely why it is treated as personal data.

## What you can do yourself

Start by checking the framing. A slightly tighter crop or a later start point often avoids uninvolved people appearing clearly at all.

If it still matters, you can blur faces and plates. What counts is that the blur tracks across the whole clip rather than sitting on a single frame; the article on [blurring faces and plates](/en/blog/blurring-faces-and-plates) describes how.

## What not to do

Change nothing about the scene itself. Blurring the plate of the other party destroys the value of the footage as evidence. For a submission to a channel, anonymising bystanders makes sense; for footage you present to your insurer or the police, the original stays untouched.

Keep those two routes separate: the original for establishing the facts, an edited version for publication.

## Audio belongs in this too

Conversations inside the vehicle enjoy particular protection and usually contribute nothing to assessing a traffic scene. If your clip contains sound, listen to it before submitting. Often the simplest solution is to remove the audio track, as the article on [audio in your clip](/en/blog/audio-in-your-clip) describes.

## When in doubt, hold back

If you are unsure whether a scene shows uninvolved people in a sensitive situation, do not submit it. Footage you keep can still be passed on tomorrow. Footage that has been published cannot be taken back.

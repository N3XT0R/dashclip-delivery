Behind a windscreen the conditions are ones few electronic devices are built for. Over seventy degrees in summer, well below freezing in winter, and changing humidity throughout. Dashcams therefore age faster than other devices.

## Heat is the biggest enemy

Direct sun on a closed vehicle produces temperatures that stress every component. Batteries suffer most, since they can swell, along with memory cards, which wear out faster.

Many cameras shut down when they overheat. That is protection rather than a fault, but it has an awkward consequence: while the device is off, nothing is recorded, and that is often exactly when the vehicle sits unattended.

To avoid it, choose a device with a capacitor rather than a battery and take the camera down during long stays in the sun. The article on [capacitor or battery](/en/blog/capacitor-or-battery) describes the differences.

## Cold costs capacity and time

In frost a camera starts more slowly, and devices with a battery lose noticeable performance. Some models record nothing at all for the first few minutes after a cold start.

That is a particular problem in winter, when many journeys are short. During the cold season, check more often that a file was actually created after a typical drive.

## Humidity and condensation

The change between a cold night and a warm cabin produces condensation, and not only on the glass but inside the housing too. It shows as fogging behind the lens, which over time can settle permanently.

> [!NOTE]
> If the lens fogs from the inside and does not clear, moisture has entered the housing. From that point on, image quality is permanently affected.

## What you can do

Mount the camera with a small gap to the glass so air can circulate. In winter, direct airflow at the windscreen, including the area behind the mirror.

Take the camera with you in high summer and hard frost when the vehicle stands for a long time. That protects the hardware and removes the incentive for a break-in.

## How to spot wear

Longer start-up times, frequent shutdowns, a distorted housing, files that will not play and a lens that looks permanently fogged. Each of those signs means the camera no longer does its job reliably.

The [monthly quick check](/en/blog/your-monthly-dashcam-check) catches most of these cases before they surface at the worst moment.

## The mount suffers too

Adhesive pads lose grip in high heat, and suction cups even more so. A camera slowly rotating downwards over the summer films the bonnet for weeks without anyone noticing.

So check the alignment occasionally in summer, not just that it works. A quick look at the last recording is enough to see whether the framing still holds.

## Cables and connectors

Temperature swings take their toll on connectors as well. A cable that expands in heat and contracts in cold works loose in its socket over time. If your camera restarts sporadically, that is one of the first places to look.

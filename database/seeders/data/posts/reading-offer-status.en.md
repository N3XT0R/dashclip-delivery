Your overview shows a state for every submission. Those states look like administration at first; in fact they answer the only question that interests you: do I need to do something, or is something happening?

## Offered and under review

The most common state. Your clip sits with a channel, the deadline is running, and the decision rests there. There is nothing for you to do, and chasing speeds nothing up.

You can also see which channel currently holds the offer and how long it still runs. There is more on this in the article on [offer deadlines](/en/blog/understanding-offer-deadlines).

## Taken on

A channel has downloaded the clip. Distribution to the other channels you selected ends there, because two channels should not work up the same scene in parallel.

Taken on does not mean published. Between the download and a finished piece sits editorial work that can take weeks. The article on [what happens after the download](/en/blog/what-happens-after-the-download) describes the process.

## Returned

A channel actively decided the clip does not suit. That is the fastest form of response, because your clip moves straight on to the next channel on your list.

A return is not a verdict on quality. The most common reasons are collected in the article on [why channels decline](/en/blog/why-channels-decline).

## Expired

No decision was taken within the deadline. The effect is the same as a return, only slower: the clip moves on.

This happens regularly and usually says something about the capacity of a channel that week rather than about your footage.

> [!NOTE]
> Once all selected channels have been through, the process ends. You can offer the same clip again afterwards, for instance to a different selection.

## When to act yourself

Really only in two cases. First, when a state does not match what you expect and does not resolve after a few days. Second, when you want to add an important detail about your clip afterwards.

In both cases a short, specific report with the date and name of the submission helps, as described in the article on [reporting problems](/en/blog/reporting-problems-and-getting-help).

## Why states exist at all

Without a visible state there would only be waiting. The states show where a clip currently sits and replace the question of whether anything is happening at all.

The same applies in reverse for channels: they see which offers are open and how much time remains. Together that keeps material from sitting unnoticed.

## One look a week is enough

You do not have to check the overview daily. The important changes reach you by email anyway, as the article on [notifications](/en/blog/keeping-notifications-useful) describes.

A weekly look is enough to stay on top of things, and it also shows you which of your channel selections actually work.

Video files are large, and a connection on the road rarely is. Anyone wanting to upload anyway gets further with a few tricks, without degrading the footage.

## Work on the clip, not on the quality

The most effective measure is trimming. A twenty second clip is a fraction of a three minute segment, and it is the better submission for a channel in any case.

What you should not do is reduce the resolution or re-encode the file. That saves transfer time and costs exactly the detail that matters. The article on [file formats](/en/blog/file-formats-and-resolution) goes into it.

## Choosing the right moment

Mobile networks are busiest in the evening. An upload late in the morning often runs noticeably faster than the same upload at eight in the evening.

If you can reach a fixed connection anyway, wait for it. Half an hour later on a stable network beats two interrupted attempts on the road.

## Stable beats fast

An upload mainly needs a connection that does not drop. During the transfer, avoid driving between cells, keep the page open and do not let the device sleep.

> [!NOTE]
> Uploading from a moving vehicle is the least reliable case. Where you can arrange it, upload while stationary.

## When it breaks off anyway

Check first whether the file arrived completely before starting a second attempt. A duplicate submission of the same scene is awkward, because it costs channels time and creates confusion in your overview.

If interruptions persist, break larger plans up: one clip first, then the next. The article on [uploading several clips](/en/blog/uploading-several-clips-at-once) describes how to group files sensibly.

## Allow for patience

On a narrow connection, expect a clip to take several minutes. That is not a fault, it is physics. Start the upload when you can let it run, not just before setting off.

## When nothing works at all

Secure the file first and upload it later. A submission that arrives three days later is still a submission. A recording overwritten because you sat in a dead spot waiting for an upload is lost. How to secure it beforehand is covered in the article on [rescuing footage](/en/blog/rescuing-footage-before-it-is-overwritten).

## How to tell where the problem sits

When an upload crawls, the connection is not always to blame. Check the file size first: an untrimmed three minute segment in high resolution easily runs to a gigabyte, and that takes time on any mobile network.

Then look at whether your device is running other transfers in the background, such as a phone backup or a system update. Both share the same line and slow the upload down.

## An approach that works

Trim the clip, copy the file to a computer, start the upload over a fixed connection and let it run. That order needs a little preparation but gets there reliably, and it costs less time overall than three attempts on the road.

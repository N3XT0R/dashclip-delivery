Almost every dashcam records sound, and on almost every one the microphone is on by default. Whether that makes sense depends less on the technology than on what you intend to do with the footage.

## What audio contributes

In some situations the soundtrack explains what the image does not show. A horn, screeching tyres, an impact outside the frame, the siren of an emergency vehicle not yet visible. In such cases audio is a genuine gain.

Placing events in time benefits too. A sound marks a moment more definitely than an image sometimes can, particularly in poor visibility.

## What speaks against it

People talk inside a vehicle. Conversations between driver and passengers enjoy particular legal protection, and they practically never contribute anything to assessing a traffic scene.

There is a practical side as well. What is said in the heat of the moment often sounds different out of context than it was meant. A soundtrack can turn otherwise clear footage into an argument about side issues.

> [!NOTE]
> If you regularly carry other people, switch the microphone off or tell them about it. That applies regardless of whether you ever pass the recordings on.

## The practical recommendation

For normal operation, leave the microphone off. Most cameras provide a button that switches audio on while driving for the rare occasion when it genuinely matters.

Alternatively, record with sound and remove the audio track before submitting. Common software does that in a few steps and leaves the image untouched.

## Listen before you submit

If your clip contains audio, listen to all of it before passing it on. Note whether names are mentioned, whether third parties can be clearly understood and whether anything is said that you would not repeat publicly.

Those two minutes save you from later wondering how a particular remark ended up in a published piece.

## Audio for authorities and insurers

If you present footage to the police or your insurer, something different applies than for publication: the original file belongs there, unchanged and complete. Remove nothing in that case, and hand over the file exactly as the camera wrote it.

Keep the two routes apart. The original stays untouched, and for a submission to a channel you create a version without sound if needed. What else to consider is covered in the article on [plates and faces](/en/blog/plates-and-faces-before-you-submit).

## Finding the setting

On most cameras the option sits under a term such as microphone, audio recording or sound. After changing it, check on a short test recording that it actually took effect, because some devices reset the setting after a factory restart.

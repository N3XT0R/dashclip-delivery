In winter dashcams work under the hardest conditions of the year. The glass fogs, there is barely any light, and a low sun glares straight into the lens. A few small habits still produce usable footage.

## Fogged glass

Condensation forms exactly where the camera is looking. While the windscreen is fogged, no setting and no resolution helps, because the lens is filming through a milky layer.

Run the windscreen heating early in winter and direct airflow at the front glass even once the cabin is warm. The spot behind the rear-view mirror is often the last to clear, because it sits in the lee of the vents.

Make sure the camera is not wedged tight against the glass. A small gap lets warm air reach the area in front of the lens as well.

## Cold and power

Camera systems with a battery lose noticeable capacity in frost. At very low temperatures some devices do not start at all or end the recording after a short while. Models with a capacitor are the more robust choice, since they draw their energy from the vehicle anyway.

> [!NOTE]
> A camera that starts slowly in frost is usually not faulty. Even so, check after the first drive that a file was actually created.

## Light that is missing, light that blinds

In winter you often drive at dusk. The camera automatics then raise sensitivity, which brightens the image and makes it noisier at the same time. A camera aimed slightly down, capturing little sky, copes better because the exposure follows the road surface.

The opposite happens with a low sun. Filmed directly into it, any footage turns flat and number plates disappear in the glare. The best remedy is clean glass: every smear scatters the light further.

## Snow, salt and visibility

Road salt settles as a film on the windscreen and clouds the image long before you notice it while driving. Washer fluid rated for frost and working wiper blades are therefore also a question of recording quality.

Check more often in winter that the camera is recording at all. Cold, short journeys and frequent starts are the combination memory cards fail on first. The [monthly quick check](/en/blog/your-monthly-dashcam-check) is particularly worthwhile at this time of year.

## Short trips are the hardest test

In winter many journeys consist of a few kilometres. For the camera that means frequent starts, barely any time to warm up, and a fresh write cycle on the memory card each time. That combination ages cards faster than long drives do.

If you mostly drive short distances in winter, check your recordings more often and be generous about replacing the card. An extra look after the first night of frost is particularly worthwhile.

## Before heading south

Anyone crossing the Alps in winter should establish in advance which rules apply along the way. In several neighbouring countries operating a dashcam is prohibited or heavily restricted, regardless of where the vehicle is registered. The article on [using a dashcam abroad](/en/blog/dashcams-abroad) brings the main points together.

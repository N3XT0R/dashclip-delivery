Getting a number plate into frame is easy. Getting a readable number plate into frame is considerably harder. Between the two sit a few physical relationships that no amount of extra resolution can override.

## Why distance changes everything

A number plate is roughly half a metre wide. For the characters on it to be legible, enough pixels have to fall across that width. Double the distance and the width in frame halves, with the pixel count dropping accordingly.

In practice that means: under twenty metres a plate is usually readable in good footage, beyond forty metres rarely. That holds regardless of how high your camera resolution is, because the limit also lies in the lens and in contrast.

## The viewing angle matters too

A plate captured at an angle from the side appears compressed. The characters run together, and legibility falls away faster than it does with distance. Recordings work best directly from behind or head on from oncoming traffic.

That is exactly why plates are so often unreadable in side impacts. The vehicle sits across the camera, and at the decisive moment the plate is barely visible.

## Movement costs sharpness

The faster something moves relative to the camera, the more it blurs. With oncoming traffic both speeds add up, and a plate draws a streak across the frame. For a vehicle travelling ahead of you at a similar speed the relative movement is small, and the characters stay put.

Shorter exposures would help but cost light. That is precisely why plates are often readable in daylight and rarely at night.

> [!NOTE]
> A single still frame is often not enough. Step through the sequence frame by frame: there are usually two or three frames in which the plate is noticeably sharper than in all the others.

## What you can influence on the camera

A moderate field of view helps more than a very wide one. Wide angle lenses capture a lot of surroundings but shrink everything in the centre of the frame, and that is where the vehicle in question sits.

A decent bitrate is the second adjustment. If compression bites during movement, the characters dissolve into blocks despite a high resolution. The article on [resolution, bitrate and frame rate](/en/blog/resolution-bitrate-frame-rate) goes into this in more detail.

The camera should be properly aligned as well: two thirds road rather than half sky means the exposure follows the road surface.

## When the plate stays unreadable

The footage is not worthless even then. Vehicle type, colour, distinctive features, driving behaviour and the sequence of events all remain visible, and combined with other details that often adds up to a clear picture.

In such cases extend your description accordingly rather than guessing at the plate. What belongs there is covered in the article on [writing the description](/en/blog/writing-the-description).

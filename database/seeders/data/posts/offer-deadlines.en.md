When you offer your clip to a channel, no permanent claim arises. The offer is valid for a limited time and lapses afterwards. What sounds like a restriction at first is the mechanism that keeps your material from sitting idle.

## Why there is a deadline at all

A channel can fail to respond for many reasons. A holiday, a full production week, a subject that does not fit right now. Without a deadline your clip would stay tied up during that time with nobody working on it.

The deadline solves that without you having to do anything. Once it passes, the clip becomes available again and can be offered to the next channel on your list.

## What happens during the deadline

The channel sees your clip, the description and the key details. It can download the clip or return it. Returning takes effect immediately and releases the clip earlier than simply letting the time run out.

Everything stays visible to you meanwhile: your overview shows which channel currently holds the offer and how long it still runs.

## After it expires

An expired offer is not a rejection in any editorial sense. It only means no decision was taken within the window. Your clip then moves on, provided you selected further channels.

Once all selected channels have been through, the process ends. You can offer the clip again afterwards, for instance to a different selection of channels, if you want to.

> [!NOTE]
> An expired offer says nothing about the quality of your clip. It says something about the capacity of that channel this week.

## Why not everyone at once

The obvious approach would be to give the clip to all selected channels immediately. That would lead to several channels working on the same scene and the same material appearing repeatedly at the end. For channels that is unattractive, and for you it means your clip loses value.

Distribution therefore runs in sequence. How exactly that works is described in the article on [automatic video distribution](/en/blog/automatic-video-distribution-dashclip-delivery).

## What you can influence

Not the deadline itself, but the starting position. A complete description and a cleanly trimmed clip shorten the time a channel needs to decide. Someone who sees immediately what a clip is about decides faster.

A focused channel selection helps too. Five suitable channels usually take you further than fifteen arbitrary ones, because the clip reaches someone who can actually use it sooner. There is more in the article on [choosing several channels](/en/blog/choosing-several-channels).

## Patience is part of it

Several days often pass between a submission and a response. That is not a sign that something is broken. Check your overview when you want to know where your clip stands, and let the deadlines do their work.

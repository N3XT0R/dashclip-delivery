Not every recording belongs with the police, and not every scene that annoyed you is worth reporting. Where footage is relevant, however, how you hand it over decides how usable it stays.

> [!WARNING]
> This article gives general orientation and is not legal advice. Whether and how to proceed in your case is a question for a lawyer if in doubt.

## When footage belongs there

The case is clear for a crash with injuries, for a hit and run and for situations where someone was seriously endangered. There the footage is part of establishing what happened.

It is less clear for everyday infringements. Footage of every vehicle that follows too closely helps nobody and ties up capacity. Anyone reporting regularly should ask whether the balance still holds.

## Hand over the original

Pass on the unchanged file exactly as the camera wrote it. No trimming, no conversion, no blurring, no editing. Any change raises the question of what else was altered.

Keep a copy of your own. Handing something over does not mean giving up your access to it.

## What to write alongside

State the date, time, place and direction of travel, plus the point in the video where the event occurs. If the camera clock deviates, say by how much. These details save follow-up questions and stop a minor discrepancy becoming a point of dispute.

Describe the sequence factually and without judgement. What you saw belongs there; what you suspect does not.

## The route for handover

Ask in which form the file is expected. A storage medium or an upload through an online portal are common. Email usually fails on file size, and shrinking the file is not an option.

Note when you handed which file to whom. That sounds excessive but helps when a question arrives months later.

## What happens afterwards

Whether footage leads to proceedings is not your decision. A recording is one piece of evidence among several and is assessed in context. Expect it to take time and expect not to be informed about every intermediate step.

Expect as well that the footage shows your own driving too. Anyone travelling well above the limit documents that along with everything else.

## And publication?

While a matter is running, publishing is not a good idea. It can prejudge those involved and affect the proceedings, and nothing can be taken back.

Wait until the matter is concluded and then check what is permissible. The article on [publishing footage](/en/blog/publishing-footage) goes into that.

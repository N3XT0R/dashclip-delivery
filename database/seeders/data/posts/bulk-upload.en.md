After a long drive or a weekend on the road, several scenes sometimes accumulate. Instead of clicking through each one separately, the bulk route pays off, provided you prepare a few things.

## When the bulk route is worthwhile

At three clips or more. Below that you are faster individually, because each file needs its own description anyway.

It also makes sense when the clips belong together, such as several scenes from the same drive or footage from two cameras showing the same incident.

## Preparation decides

Trim each clip beforehand to what you want to show. A bulk upload of untrimmed segments creates work that nobody will sort out afterwards.

Name the files traceably, ideally with date, time and a keyword. That helps you match the descriptions and prevents two similar scenes being mixed up.

> [!NOTE]
> Each clip remains its own submission with its own description and its own channel selection. The bulk route saves the uploading, not the thinking.

## Archive files

If you want to transfer many files at once, an archive can simplify the process, because one transfer runs instead of many. Create the archive without additional compression: video files can barely be compressed further anyway, and the processing gains nothing.

Skip passwords and nested folder structures. Both make further handling needlessly complicated.

## The descriptions

Even in a bulk upload, every clip needs a place, a time and one line about the situation. Write those details into a short list while you trim the clips. Filling them in afterwards then takes a few minutes of routine work.

What belongs in a good description is covered in the article on [writing the description](/en/blog/writing-the-description).

## Channel selection per clip

Resist the temptation to give every clip the same channel selection. A city traffic scene and a motorway scene rarely suit the same channels.

Take the minute per clip. It influences the outcome more than anything else, as the article on [choosing several channels](/en/blog/choosing-several-channels) describes.

## After uploading

Check in your overview that all clips arrived and that each carries the right description. With a larger batch a mix-up happens easily, and it otherwise only surfaces when a channel asks.

## Splitting large batches

When ten or more clips accumulate, split them into two or three rounds. That keeps the transfers short, and an interruption does not cost you all your progress.

Sort them by subject rather than chronologically. All city scenes in one round, all motorway scenes in the next: the channel selection then fits within a round as well, and you work noticeably faster.

## What to filter out beforehand

Not every recording that caught your eye will carry a submission. Scenes without a recognisable sequence, footage in heavy rain with no visibility and clips where essentially nothing happens cost you time in describing them and are declined afterwards.

Filter those out before uploading. Two good clips are worth more than eight mediocre ones, as the article on [what makes a good submission](/en/blog/what-makes-a-good-submission) explains.

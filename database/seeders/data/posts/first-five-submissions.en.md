Your first submissions decide less about success than about whether you develop a routine. Anyone who approaches the first five deliberately later does in minutes what initially takes half an hour.

## The first: the walkthrough

Choose an easy clip on purpose. A clear situation, good light, short duration. The point is not to submit your best material but to run through the whole process once.

Take your time over this first submission and watch what happens after you send it. The article on [your first upload](/en/blog/your-first-upload) describes the steps.

## The second: the trim

Now concentrate on the section. Ten seconds of lead-in, five seconds afterwards, no cut in the middle of a movement.

Watch the finished section once as though you had never been there. If you find yourself guessing anywhere, the clip starts too late.

## The third: the description

Place, date, time, one line about the situation and what you did yourself. No more, but no less either.

Write only what you know for certain. Estimated speeds and assumptions about intent have no place in a description, as the article on [writing the description](/en/blog/writing-the-description) explains.

## The fourth: the channel selection

So far you may have taken whatever was obvious. Now look at three channels more closely: what do they publish, how do they handle people in frame, how long are their pieces?

Then choose three to five channels that genuinely fit. Why that works better than a long list is covered in the article on [choosing several channels](/en/blog/choosing-several-channels).

## The fifth: reading the responses

By now your first submissions have reached states: taken on, returned, expired. Look at what happened and search for a pattern.

If several channels decline for the same reason, the material is the issue. If the reasons differ, the selection is. The article on [reading offer status](/en/blog/reading-offer-status) explains the states.

## Afterwards

From the sixth submission on it runs alongside everything else: secure, trim, describe, select, send. What remains is the one habit that carries all of it: secure the recording before the loop overwrites it.

## What to note while you do it

For each of the first five submissions, record three things: which clip you sent, which channels you selected and what came of it.

That note soon replaces any general recommendation. It shows you which channels suit your material, and that depends heavily on where and when you drive.

## The most common beginner mistakes

A section that starts too late, an empty description, too many channels at once and footage that was edited before being submitted. All four can be fixed before sending.

The fifth mistake happens earlier: not securing the recording while it still exists. Only habit helps against that.

Taking your own camera into a rental car makes sense: in an unfamiliar vehicle on unfamiliar roads, a recording is useful. Three things are worth settling beforehand.

## The rental agreement

Many agreements contain clauses about attachments and modifications to the vehicle. A suction cup on the windscreen is usually unproblematic; an adhesive pad or wiring inside the cabin is not.

So stick to a mount that comes off without residue and do not route cables behind trim. The accessory socket is the right route here, even if the cable stays visible.

## The local rules

This is the more important point. A rental car normally sits wherever you have just arrived, and rules on video recording differ considerably from country to country.

In Austria, Switzerland, Luxembourg and Portugal operating a dashcam is heavily restricted or prohibited. What counts is the law of the country you are driving in, not where you live. The article on [using a dashcam abroad](/en/blog/dashcams-abroad) collects the details.

> [!NOTE]
> In countries with a ban: take the camera down and stow it rather than merely switching it off. A mounted camera creates a need for explanation during a check.

## Documenting damage

With a rental car, which damage was already there is a frequent dispute. A dashcam is the wrong tool for that, though: it films forwards, not your own vehicle.

Photograph the car all round at pickup and return instead, including wheels, roof and interior, and make sure the date is visible. Those images help you far more reliably than any video.

## The camera at the end of the rental

Take the camera down before returning the car and check whether any residue is left on the glass. Remember the memory card as well: it belongs to you, not to the vehicle.

If you leave the camera in the car during your trip, stow it out of sight. A visible device is a reason for a break-in, and in a rental car you carry that risk too.

## If something does happen

Secure the footage immediately before the loop overwrites it, and report the damage to the rental company without delay. How to secure the file is described in the article on [rescuing footage](/en/blog/rescuing-footage-before-it-is-overwritten).

## Do not forget the clock

After a flight you may be in a different time zone. Set the camera accordingly before setting off, otherwise every recording carries a time that does not match its surroundings.

Cameras with position reception often take the time automatically, but they need a clear view of the sky and a few minutes. Check it after the first drive.

## A word on insurance

Before renting, establish the excess and whether additional cover makes sense. Footage can show how damage occurred, but it does not replace cover.

Claims handling abroad in particular takes time. So note the time, place and everyone involved carefully, and hand the footage over unchanged as described in the article on [submitting footage to insurers](/en/blog/submitting-footage-to-insurers).

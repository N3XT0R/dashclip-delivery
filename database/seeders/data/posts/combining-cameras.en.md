When two cameras have recorded the same situation, a fuller picture emerges. For that to become a usable submission, however, the recordings have to line up, and that is less automatic than it sounds.

## Check the clocks first

The most common stumbling block is two cameras showing different times. Even two minutes of deviation is enough for nobody to be able to tell whether it is the same scene.

Compare the timestamps before combining anything and note the difference. Once you know it, you can state it in the description rather than altering the files.

## Two routes for submitting

The first route: submit both clips as separate submissions and note in both descriptions that they show the same incident from different perspectives.

The second route: place both perspectives side by side in one file so they are visible at the same time. That is more vivid but means intervening in the material.

For submissions to channels the first route is usually better. Channels edit themselves anyway and prefer raw material, as the article on [file formats](/en/blog/file-formats-and-resolution) describes.

## If you do combine them

Make sure both recordings genuinely run in sync. A one second offset is enough for the two perspectives to appear to contradict each other.

Skip captions, arrows and slow motion. Whatever you add usually has to be removed again at the channel, and it reads as an interpretation of events.

> [!NOTE]
> Keep both original files unchanged in any case. For insurers and authorities they belong handed over individually, not as a combined version.

## Front and rear tell different stories

The front camera shows what you were driving towards, the rear camera what happened behind you. In a rear-end collision the rear recording is the decisive one; in a lane change the front one is.

So choose deliberately rather than sending both perspectives automatically. Footage that contributes nothing to understanding only lengthens the review.

## Footage from other vehicles

Occasionally another vehicle recorded the same scene. You cannot simply submit such footage as your own, because it belongs to someone else.

Instead, point out that another recording exists and leave the decision to the person who made it.

## File names help more than memory

Name recordings that belong together identically, with a suffix for the perspective such as front and rear. When a question arrives weeks later, you find both files immediately.

Store them in the same folder as well. Perspectives filed separately are the most common reason only one of them turns up later. How to organise your material overall is covered in the article on [your clip archive](/en/blog/your-clip-archive).

## In short

Compare the clocks, note the deviation, leave the originals unchanged, submit separately and mention the second perspective in the description. That gives a channel or an insurer everything they need.

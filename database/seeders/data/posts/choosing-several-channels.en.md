When submitting you can select several channels. The obvious strategy is to tick as many as possible. In practice a considered selection gets you there faster.

## What happens with your selection

The selected channels form a pool. Your clip is offered from it one channel at a time, not to everyone at once. Each channel gets its deadline, and only when it returns the clip or lets the deadline pass does the next one get a turn.

Something important follows from that: order and fit decide how long it takes. A pool of fifteen channels, twelve of which do not fit the subject, keeps your clip occupied for weeks before it reaches someone who can use it.

## Quality beats quantity

Three to five well matched channels are usually the better choice. They lead to a decision sooner, and the likelihood of the clip being taken on is higher than with a broad spread.

To judge that, look at what a channel publishes, the tone it works in and how it handles people in frame. The article on [discovering channels](/en/blog/discovering-channels) describes what to look for.

> [!NOTE]
> You can offer a recording again later once all selected channels have been through. A small first selection therefore rules nothing out.

## A sensible order

Put the channels where your clip fits best at the front. Those may be channels that cover exactly this subject regularly, or ones that have decided promptly in the past.

At the back go the channels where you can imagine it fitting but are not sure. That way you lose no time if the ones in front take the clip.

## When a broad selection makes sense

There is one case where many channels do make sense: when your clip shows a very general subject that suits many formats, such as a typical hazard without distinctive features.

For material that dates quickly a broader selection can help too, because every deadline counts.

## What you cannot influence

Whether a channel takes a clip depends on its planning, not only on your footage. A rejection often means similar material is already in hand. How the deadlines work is described in the article on [offer deadlines](/en/blog/understanding-offer-deadlines).

Stick to your approach with the next submission: select carefully, describe clearly, then wait. Across several clips a pattern emerges that shows which channels suit your material.

## What to note down

Record which channels you selected for which kind of clip and how it turned out. After five or six submissions you will see where your material regularly lands.

That note is worth more than any general recommendation, because it matches what you actually record. Someone filming mostly city traffic needs a different selection than someone driving country roads.

## When you are unsure

When in doubt, choose fewer channels and pick the ones you are most confident about. A second round is always possible, and the clip loses nothing by waiting.

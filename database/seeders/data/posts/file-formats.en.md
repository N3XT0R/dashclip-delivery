Before uploading there is often an urge to optimise the file first: shrink it, convert it, put it into another format. In most cases that degrades the footage without gaining anything.

## Why the original is the best format

Dashcams usually save in a format that opens practically everywhere. That file is already compressed, exactly once. Every further conversion compresses again and throws away detail that cannot be recovered.

Fine structures suffer first, which is exactly what matters: the characters on a number plate, the angle of the wheels, an indicator lighting up in a dark scene.

> [!NOTE]
> If you only need a section, trim without re-encoding. Many tools offer a lossless cut that carries the image data through unchanged.

## Do not reduce the resolution

Scaling a high resolution recording down to a smaller size feels practical because the file shrinks. For channels it is a loss: a crop can be enlarged out of high resolution footage, but not out of a downscaled copy.

Leave the aspect ratio alone as well. If a channel needs a different format, for instance for viewing on a phone, it will crop the material itself and decide which part of the frame matters.

## Keeping file size manageable

Large files are no problem as long as the connection is stable. If the upload becomes a test of patience, a tighter trim helps far more than a conversion: twenty seconds at original quality is smaller than three minutes at half quality, and worth more.

Avoid archive formats that wrap the content up again. A single video file can be checked immediately, whereas an archive has to be unpacked first. Several clips at once are a separate case, covered in the article on [uploading several clips](/en/blog/uploading-several-clips-at-once).

## Do not lose the metadata

The original file carries the recording time and, depending on the camera, further details. Many conversions discard that information. Since time and place are among the most important details of a submission, this is another reason to leave the file as it is. There is more in the article on [time and place](/en/blog/time-and-place).

In short: upload what the camera wrote, trim it in time at most, and leave the rest to the channels.

## When the camera writes an unusual format

Some devices save into a proprietary container that only opens with the bundled software, or store extra data in separate files. In that case, export the recording once into a common video format and keep the original alongside it.

Exporting is the exception to the rule here, because a file nobody can open is worthless as a submission. Make sure to pick the highest quality the export offers and leave the resolution unchanged.

## A short checklist before uploading

Does the file play on a device other than the one it came from? Does the length of the clip match what you want to show? Is the resolution unchanged? Are the time details still present, either in the image or in the metadata?

Four questions, one minute of effort, and the file is as good as your camera can deliver.

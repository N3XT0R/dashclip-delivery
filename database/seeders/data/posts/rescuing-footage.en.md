The recording loop knows no exceptions. It overwrites the oldest segment as soon as the card is full, regardless of what was on it. Anyone who wants to keep a recording has to act, and faster than most people assume.

## How much time you have

That depends on card capacity and bitrate. A 64 gigabyte card holds roughly eight to twelve hours of driving at typical settings. Someone commuting daily has therefore lost their recording within two to three days.

With smaller cards or high settings the window can be considerably shorter. When in doubt, count on a day rather than a week.

## The fastest route

Switch the camera off. Take the card out. The recording is then safe, because nothing can be overwritten without a card. That sounds crude, but it is the only method that always works regardless of camera model, menu structure and button layout.

If you carry a second card, insert it and drive on with a working camera. That is exactly why a spare card in the glovebox pays off.

## The protection button route

Almost every camera has a button that protects the current segment. Protected files are excluded from the loop and stay until you delete them or format the card.

Check afterwards that the protection was actually applied. Some cameras indicate it only briefly, and a button press that did not register otherwise goes unnoticed until the file is gone.

> [!NOTE]
> Protection covers only the segment currently running, and often the one immediately before. If the event lies further back, only removing the card helps.

## Backing up on a computer

Copy the files unchanged to a computer and work only with copies afterwards. Do not move the originals, do not rename them and do not open them in a program that saves on closing.

Store the backup in two places, for instance on the computer and on external storage. A single copy is not a backup.

## When the file is already overwritten

Then it is usually lost. Recovery tools can occasionally salvage fragments from memory cards, but with a camera that kept running after the event the affected areas have typically been rewritten several times over.

Only one thing matters in that case: take the card out of the camera the moment you notice the loss. Every further minute of recording reduces the chance further.

## The habit that helps

Make it a rule not to drive on after a notable event without having secured the recording. The order to follow is described in the article on [after a crash](/en/blog/after-a-crash-securing-the-footage).

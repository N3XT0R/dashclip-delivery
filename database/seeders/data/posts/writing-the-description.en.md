The description is the shortest part of a submission and the one most often left empty. Yet it often decides whether a channel opens the clip at all. A video without context is, to the person reviewing it, just one file among many.

## Four details that always belong

Place, date, time and one line about the situation. At its core nothing more is needed. The location can stay approximate, such as a town and a street or a motorway section with direction of travel. Date and time ideally come from the recording itself.

The line about the situation answers a single question: what can be seen? One sentence is enough. Writing a paragraph instead only postpones the work, because the scene itself is in the video anyway.

## What you did yourself

Describe your own actions neutrally. Whether you braked, swerved, sounded the horn or simply carried on helps a viewer understand the scene. This is not a justification, it is information the image does not always show.

It is particularly valuable for evasive manoeuvres. From the camera perspective a steering movement sometimes looks milder than it was, because the frame swings with it.

## Naming the unusual

Anything that explains the situation but is hard to see in the image belongs in the description. A wet road, glare from a low sun, roadworks outside the frame, a vehicle that changed lanes shortly before and has since left the picture.

Mention technical quirks too. If your camera overlays a timestamp that is two minutes out, write it down. That takes any later discussion off the table.

> [!NOTE]
> Write only what you actually know. Estimated speeds and assumed intentions read as facts once they sit in a description field, and they are hard to walk back afterwards.

## What you can leave out

Judgements about the other people involved contribute nothing. A channel assesses the scene itself and uses its own words. Details of your camera equipment are equally unnecessary, unless they explain why the image looks the way it does.

Leave out names and addresses of those involved as well, even if you know them. They are not needed to assess the footage, and a description field is not the place for them.

## A workable example

Two sentences are enough: A road between Wittlich and Bitburg, 14 March, 07:42, dense fog. A vehicle overtakes before a crest, I brake and pull to the right.

With that, anyone reviewing knows where to look, under which conditions the recording was made and what is about to happen. A clip rarely needs more context than that. How to choose the matching section is covered in the article on [choosing the right section](/en/blog/choosing-the-right-section).

## Adding something later

If something occurs to you after submitting, add it promptly. The earlier the information exists, the more likely it reaches the channel before the review. Read a week later, it usually changes nothing.

Keep your own note about the clip as well. When a question arrives months later you will remember the scene, but rarely the exact date and time.

Notifications are the part of a platform nobody thinks about while they work. Without them you miss deadlines. With too many you stop reading them. A look at what is sent and why helps put them in place.

## When you receive a message

In principle whenever something changes about one of your submissions that requires a decision or your awareness. A channel has taken your clip on. An offer was returned. An offer expired and your clip moves on.

Alongside those are messages about your account: confirming your address, changes to security relevant settings, notes about processes you triggered yourself.

## Why you cannot unsubscribe

These messages are part of operating the service. Someone who receives no response about a running offer cannot know whether to act. That is exactly why they are not advertising and not optional.

The counterpart is restraint: messages are sent only when there is a concrete reason. A message with nothing to act on is not a good message, it is a superfluous one.

## Making sure they arrive

After the first message, take a quick look in your spam folder. If you find it there, move it to your inbox and mark the sender as trusted. Subsequent messages then land where they belong.

Use an address you actually read. A catch-all address you open once a month leads to deadlines expiring while the relevant message sits unread.

> [!NOTE]
> Check the stored address after every change of mail provider. An address that no longer exists goes unnoticed for a long time, because nothing comes back that you would see.

## The language of messages

Notifications follow the language set in your account. If you change it, the new language applies to all future messages. Content from other people, such as channel descriptions, stays in its original language.

## When nothing arrives

Check three things in this order. First: is the address in your profile correct? Second: is the message in your spam folder? Third: is a filter at your provider blocking the sender?

If it persists, a report helps. Which details are useful is covered in the article on [reporting problems](/en/blog/reporting-problems-and-getting-help).

## Your overview stays the reliable source

Independently of email, the current state is always in your overview. It shows which channel currently holds an offer and how long it still runs. People who submit regularly get into the habit of looking there instead of waiting for a message.

## One good habit

Create a folder or label for messages about your submissions and let a rule sort them there automatically. Your inbox stays readable and you can see at a glance which processes are running.

Archive messages about finished submissions rather than deleting them. When a question about a clip arrives months later, you find the date, the channel and the sequence again within seconds.

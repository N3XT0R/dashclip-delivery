Choosing channels is the step where you have the most influence over the outcome. Everything before it is craft, everything after it runs automatically. It is worth knowing the channels you offer your clip to.

## Channels have a signature

Two channels can treat the same subject in completely different ways. One shows traffic situations plainly with matter-of-fact commentary, the other works with sharper edges and humour. Footage that suits the first feels out of place on the second, and the other way round.

So look at what a channel has published so far. Which kind of scenes appear there regularly? How long are the pieces? Are number plates blurred? Those observations say more about fit than any description does.

## What suits your clip

Ask yourself what your recording actually shows. Is it a typical hazard others can learn something from? An unusual one-off? A scene that is mainly interesting because of how it ends?

Channels that focus on road safety look for different material than those collecting oddities. The more clearly you can name what your clip is about, the easier the choice becomes.

> [!NOTE]
> A channel that fits thematically may still decline if it currently holds a lot of similar material. That is not a verdict on your recording.

## Less is often more

Selecting as many channels as possible feels like the obvious move. In practice a deliberate selection produces better results. Channels notice whether material suits them, and a clip offered everywhere at once rarely looks like a considered decision.

Choose the channels where you can genuinely picture your footage finding a place. How distribution proceeds afterwards, and why not everyone can access a clip at the same time, is explained in the article on [automatic video distribution](/en/blog/automatic-video-distribution-dashclip-delivery).

## Take your time

You do not have to decide immediately. Look through the channels at your leisure and note two or three that suit the kind of material you tend to capture. With your next clip the choice then takes seconds.

More on selecting several channels at once is in the article on [choosing several channels](/en/blog/choosing-several-channels).

## How to recognise a suitable channel

Three signals help you judge. First, regularity: a channel that has published nothing for months is unlikely to review your material promptly either. Second, how people in frame are handled: whether faces and plates are blurred says something about how carefully the work is done. Third, the treatment: is a scene put into context or merely shown?

These points are not quality verdicts, they are indications of working style. You should be comfortable with what happens to your material, because it is published under the name of the channel rather than yours.

A windscreen is not an arbitrary mounting surface. Where a camera sits has consequences for your view, for image quality and for whether the installation is acceptable.

## Your view comes first

The most important criterion is not the footage but what you can see while driving. A device sitting in your field of view is mounted wrongly, whatever its image quality.

The area directly behind the rear-view mirror has therefore proven itself. The camera obscures nothing extra there, because the mirror occupies that area anyway.

## The wiper area counts

The camera should film through the part of the glass the wipers clear. Otherwise that section stays smeared in rain, and the footage becomes unusable exactly when conditions are already difficult.

Test it once in practice: use the washers, then watch the recording. If streaks remain in the image, the camera sits too far outside the swept area.

## Distance from the glass

A small gap helps against fogging, because warm air can circulate. Too much distance brings reflections into the image: bright objects on the dashboard, a display or an interior light then appear as a ghost image.

A dark dashboard and a clear area in front of it solve that more reliably than any setting.

> [!NOTE]
> Think about cable routing. A cable belongs behind the trim, clear of airbags. The article on [mounting and alignment](/en/blog/mounting-and-alignment) describes the route.

## Sensors and special areas

On many vehicles the area behind the mirror holds the rain sensor, the light sensor and camera systems for driver assistance. Those surfaces must not be covered, or the assistance systems misbehave.

Watch out as well for heated sections of glass and for antenna structures in the windscreen. A mount on those holds less well and can impair their function.

## Alignment after fitting

Two thirds road, one third sky. The bonnet may be just visible along the bottom edge, because it helps in judging distances.

Check the result after a real drive rather than while parked. Only in motion do you see whether the mount twists over bumps and whether the framing holds up.

## After a windscreen replacement

When the windscreen is replaced, the camera has to be refitted and realigned afterwards. That is regularly forgotten, and the footage then shows the sky or the bonnet.

## When only a poor position is available

Some vehicles leave little room: steeply raked windscreens, large sensor areas, a rear-view mirror mounted low. The order then runs: your own view first, the wiper area second, the perfect perspective last.

A camera sitting slightly too far right but filming clear glass is better than a centrally mounted one looking through an unswept corner.

## A short test after fitting

Drive a familiar route, watch the footage and check three things: is the course of the road fully in frame, are the plates of vehicles ahead readable, and do reflections intrude?

If all three hold, the position is right, and you will not have to think about it for years.

A rejection feels personal and almost never is. Channels decide by criteria that relate only partly to your footage. Knowing the most common reasons lets you improve the next clip deliberately.

## The lead-in is missing

By far the most common editorial reason. The clip starts at the moment of the event, which removes exactly the information showing how it came about. Without a lead-in, nobody can judge who did what.

The fix: start roughly ten seconds before the event. There is more in the article on [choosing the right section](/en/blog/choosing-the-right-section).

## The scene is not legible

Too dark, too unsteady, too far away, obscured by a wiper. Some of that cannot be changed, other parts can: clean glass, correct alignment and a sufficient bitrate resolve a large share of these cases.

## The subject was just covered

A channel that published a piece on rear-end collisions in queues two weeks ago does not need another scene of the same kind this week. That rejection says nothing about your footage, only about the timing.

Nothing helps here except a different channel selection or offering it again later.

## The context is missing

Footage without a description creates work. If nobody knows where and when a scene happened, the channel either has to ask or leave the clip alone. In practice the second happens more often.

> [!NOTE]
> A complete description is the part you can influence most easily, and the one with the biggest effect.

## Legal concerns

If uninvolved people are clearly identifiable, shown in a sensitive situation, or the footage could not be published without extensive editing, a careful channel will decline.

That is a good sign. It means not everything that arrives there gets used.

## What you cannot change

A channel planning, its capacity this week and its editorial focus. Those factors decide a rejection more often than the quality of the recording does.

So it is worth looking at the pattern across several submissions rather than at individual answers. If several channels decline for the same reason, the material is the issue. If the reasons differ, the selection is.

## How to handle a rejection

Treat it as information rather than a verdict. Where a reason is given, note it down. Across several submissions that builds a list telling you more than any general recommendation.

Ask if you are unsure, but sparingly and factually. A channel that works with submissions regularly is usually happy to give a pointer when the question is specific.

## What happens after a rejection

Your clip is not finished. It is offered to the next channel on your list, and that channel decides independently. Only once all selected channels have been through does the process end, and even then you can offer the clip again.

How distribution works is described in the article on [automatic video distribution](/en/blog/automatic-video-distribution-dashclip-delivery).

Uploading a video means letting something out of your hands. The question of who can see it afterwards and what happens to it should be answerable without a long search. This article collects how access is arranged.

## Who can see your clip

Your clip is visible to the channels you selected yourself, and even to them only when their turn comes in the distribution. Channels you did not select do not see it.

An upload is therefore not a publication. Between uploading and a public piece sit your selection, a decision by a channel and that channel editorial work.

## What a channel receives

A channel sees the clip, your description and the key details of the submission, plus the name in your profile. Which other channels you selected is not part of that.

Once a channel downloads the clip, distribution to others ends. What follows is described in the article on [what happens after the download](/en/blog/what-happens-after-the-download).

## Your account and your data

Your account holds the details you entered yourself and the history of your submissions. That data serves the operation: without it neither attribution nor responses could be organised.

Your display name travels with your submissions. If you would rather not appear under your full name, choose it accordingly before making your first submission.

> [!NOTE]
> Check your profile before submitting for the first time. A name can be changed later, but offers already running keep the state they had when you sent them.

## What stays in your hands

The most important lever sits before the upload: what is visible in your clip is your decision. Bystanders in frame, audio from inside the cabin and location details can all be checked beforehand.

The articles on [plates and faces](/en/blog/plates-and-faces-before-you-submit), on [audio in your clip](/en/blog/audio-in-your-clip) and on [location data](/en/blog/location-data) go through each point.

## Securing access

A password you use nowhere else is the most effective protection for your account. Anyone working on shared computers should sign out after uploading.

Make sure as well that the stored address is current. It is not only the route for responses but also for everything concerning the security of your account.

## If you want to withdraw a submission

As long as a clip has not been taken on by a channel, a submission can be stopped. Once it has been downloaded, the material sits with the channel, and withdrawing it is no longer a technical question but a conversation.

That is why a careful look before sending is worth more than any later correction. Check the framing, the description and the channel selection at your leisure; after that the process largely runs itself.

## What happens to older submissions

Completed processes stay visible in your overview so you can trace which clip went to which channel and when. That history is meant for you and is not visible to other users.

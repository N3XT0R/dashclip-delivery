Almost every dashcam has an impact sensor. It detects a shock and protects the current recording from being overwritten later. That sounds like a feature you switch on once and then forget. Which is exactly what leads to the two most common problems.

## Too sensitive: the full card

With sensitivity set high, the sensor triggers on every pothole, every kerb and every hard braking manoeuvre. Each of those recordings is protected and thereby excluded from the loop.

After a few weeks the card consists mostly of protected files. The camera can no longer overwrite anything and, in the worst case, stops recording altogether. Exactly when it matters, no storage is left.

## Not sensitive enough: the missing file

Set too low, the sensor does not trigger on light contact. That is precisely what happens with car park bumps, with scrapes from a passing vehicle and with anything that unfolds without a distinct jolt.

The recording still exists at that moment, but it is not protected. If you drive on, the loop overwrites it within hours. Anyone noticing the damage the next morning finds nothing.

## The middle setting as a starting point

Start on the middle level and watch for a week how many protected files appear. Several per journey means the setting is too high. Not a single one over several days, despite driving over cobblestones, means it is too low.

Take your vehicle into account. A firmly damped suspension transmits shocks more clearly than a comfortable one, so the same setting behaves differently.

> [!NOTE]
> The sensor does not recognise a near miss. If nothing strikes anything, nothing triggers. For those situations you need the manual protection button, and you should be able to find it without looking.

## Being able to protect manually

Get familiar with the button before you need it. Take the time once to press it while parked and then check that the file was actually marked. Some cameras acknowledge it only with a short tone that is easily lost in road noise.

One caveat: press it only when the situation allows. The recording stays available for several minutes, and your attention belongs to the road first.

## Clearing out protected files

Protected recordings stay until you delete them or format the card. Go through them roughly monthly, copy out what you want to keep, then format in the camera.

That frees the card, the loop runs normally again, and the sensor can do its job. The article on [memory cards](/en/blog/memory-cards-for-dashcams) explains why formatting belongs in the camera rather than on a computer.

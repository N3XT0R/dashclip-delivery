The legal position on dashcams stops at the border. What is possible in Germany under conditions can trigger a fine in a neighbouring country, regardless of where your vehicle is registered. What counts is the law of the country you are in.

> [!WARNING]
> This article offers general orientation and is not legal advice. Check the current rules of your destination and of every country you travel through before setting off.

## Countries with severe restrictions

Austria, Luxembourg, Portugal and Switzerland treat the operation of a dashcam very restrictively. There the camera is essentially regarded as impermissible video surveillance of public space.

For Austria, the motoring association ÖAMTC sets out the [admissibility of dashcams](https://www.oeamtc.at/thema/vorschriften-strafen/zulaessigkeit-von-dashcams-im-oesterreichischen-strassenverkehr-16180594) accordingly. Fines there can be substantial and reach a five figure amount for repeated breaches. High sums are cited for Portugal as well.

One distinction matters: what is affected is operating the camera, not owning it. A camera in the glovebox is no problem; a running camera on the windscreen can be.

## Countries where private use is possible

In Germany, France, Italy, Spain and the Scandinavian countries private use is possible subject to conditions. The conditions resemble each other: no permanent recording without cause, no unlimited storage, no publication without checking first.

Do not assume a rule stays in place. Individual countries have changed their practice in recent years, in both directions.

## The safe rule for travelling

Three points are enough to stay on the safe side.

First, run only a short recording loop so nothing is stored permanently. Second, switch the microphone off. Third, publish nothing recorded along the way until you have checked the legal position.

In countries with a ban a fourth point applies: take the camera down and put it away rather than merely switching it off. A camera mounted on the windscreen creates a need for explanation during a check that you can easily avoid.

## After a crash abroad

Here things get complicated. Footage recorded unlawfully in the country you are visiting may still be considered as evidence there, or may not, depending on the legal system. At the same time the recording itself can be penalised.

So abroad, do not rely on the camera but on what works everywhere: photographs of the final positions, notes on time and place, the details of everyone involved and the European accident statement.

## Before you leave

Look at the rules for every country you cross, not only the destination. A trip to Italy runs through Austria or Switzerland, and the law of each applies in turn.

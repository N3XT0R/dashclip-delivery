Parking mode is the feature that promises most at the point of sale and raises most questions in use. The camera is meant to record while the vehicle is parked, documenting damage nobody else witnesses.

## How parking mode works

There are essentially two approaches. With motion detection the camera analyses the image and starts recording as soon as something changes. With impact triggered operation the camera rests and only wakes when the sensor registers a shock.

Both have weaknesses. Motion detection also responds to shadows, rain and passers-by, filling the card with irrelevant recordings. Impact triggered operation only starts at the moment of contact, so the lead-in is missing unless the camera keeps a buffer running.

## Nothing works without power

Parking mode needs a permanent supply. A capacitor is not enough for it, and a battery lasts minutes at best. In practice the route goes through hardwiring with a low voltage cutoff or through a separate auxiliary battery.

> [!WARNING]
> Without low voltage protection a dashcam can drain the vehicle battery to the point where the car will not start. That safeguard is a prerequisite, not an extra feature.

## The legal side

This is where the real limit sits. A camera that films public space continuously while parked is hard to justify under data protection law. It captures passers-by, neighbours and passing vehicles without any specific cause.

Far more defensible is operation that records briefly on an impact and overwrites the footage again soon afterwards. That matches the idea of data minimisation, which the article on [dashcams and data protection](/en/blog/dashcams-and-data-protection) covers in more detail.

On private ground, such as your own garage, the situation is more relaxed, because few uninvolved people are captured there.

## What parking mode does not do

It prevents no damage. At best it provides an indication of who caused it, and only when light, angle and distance cooperate. At night in an unlit parking space the prospect of a readable plate is slim.

Expect as well that a collision during manoeuvring often happens outside the frame. A front camera does not see what happens behind.

## Sensible settings

Set the sensitivity so a firm impact triggers it but a passing lorry does not. Limit the length of the recordings and let them return into the regular loop.

Check regularly that files are actually created in parking mode. That check is easily forgotten, because you only notice the result when damage occurs, and by then it is too late. The [monthly quick check](/en/blog/your-monthly-dashcam-check) collects what to look at.

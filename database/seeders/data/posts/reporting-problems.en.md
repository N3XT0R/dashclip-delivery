Sooner or later something jams: an upload breaks off, an expected message never arrives, an offer shows a state you did not expect. A good problem report shortens the diagnosis considerably, and it consists of a few concrete details.

## What belongs in a report

What were you trying to do? What happened instead? When was that? Those three questions carry half the diagnosis.

Add what you saw on screen. An error message quoted word for word is worth more than a paraphrase, because many messages differ by a single word that makes the difference.

## Details that are often missing

Which browser and which device you used. Whether you were online over mobile data or a fixed connection. Whether the error happened once or can be reproduced.

That last point matters most. An error that can be reproduced is usually fixed quickly. An error that happened once and never again needs considerably more context.

> [!NOTE]
> State the time and your approximate time zone. That alone allows processes to be matched up, even when you can supply nothing else.

## What you can check yourself first

For uploads: is the file complete, does it play locally, was the connection stable? The article on [uploading over slow connections](/en/blog/uploading-over-slow-connections) covers the most common causes.

For missing messages: is the stored address correct, is the message in your spam folder, is a filter blocking the sender? The article on [notifications](/en/blog/keeping-notifications-useful) goes into it.

For an unexpected offer state: has the deadline passed, did a channel return the clip, has it moved on? The article on [offer deadlines](/en/blog/understanding-offer-deadlines) explains the states.

## What not to include

Credentials. No legitimate route to diagnosing a problem asks for your password, and a report is the wrong place for it.

Complete video files do not belong in a first report either. Describe which submission is affected instead, and keep the file ready in case it is needed.

## After the report

Note when you sent the report and what it referred to. If a question comes back, matching it up is then immediate.

And if the problem resolves itself in the meantime, that is useful information too. An error that disappears is not the same as an error that was fixed.

## An example that works

Yesterday around 19:30 I tried to upload a clip of roughly 400 megabytes. The transfer ran to about eighty percent and then stopped with a message saying the session had expired. I used a current browser on a desktop over a fixed connection. The second attempt failed at the same point.

That is five sentences containing everything: the time, the file size, the progress, the wording of the message, the environment and the fact that the error is reproducible.

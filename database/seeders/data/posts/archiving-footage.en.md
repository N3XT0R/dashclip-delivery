Running a dashcam means producing material constantly and ideally keeping almost none of it. That sounds contradictory, but it is the core of a defensible approach to footage.

## The loop is the normal case

In normal operation you store nothing permanently. The recording loop overwrites continuously, and after a day or two an uneventful drive has gone. That is precisely the intention, as the article on [loop recording](/en/blog/understanding-loop-recording) describes.

Anything you take out of that loop needs a reason. A collision is a reason, a near miss can be one, an ordinary drive is not.

## How long to keep it

As long as the reason exists. For a claim that means until it has been settled, and a while afterwards in case questions arise.

For footage you submitted, the reason ends with the decision of the channel. For footage that came to nothing, it ends even sooner.

> [!NOTE]
> Set a reminder rather than relying on memory. A folder of recordings from the year before last does not come about deliberately, it comes about through forgetting.

## A backup is not an archive

Secured footage should sit in two places, such as on your computer and on external storage. That is protection against technical failure and not a reason to keep more.

Check once a year that the secured files still open. External storage fails silently, and you otherwise notice at the wrong moment.

## Keep originals and edits apart

Store the unchanged original separately from any edited version. For insurers and authorities only the original counts; for publication, the anonymised version does.

Anyone filing both in the same folder eventually reaches for the wrong file. The article on [your clip archive](/en/blog/your-clip-archive) describes a layout that prevents it.

## Deleting is part of it

Deleting is not a loss, it is the close of a process. Keeping footage without a reason is neither necessary nor justifiable, and if challenged you would have to explain why you collected material about other people.

Tie the clear-out to a fixed date, such as the [monthly quick check](/en/blog/your-monthly-dashcam-check). The archive then stays small and the question of justification never arises.

## While proceedings are running

As long as a matter is with an insurer, an authority or a court, the original file belongs kept unchanged. Delete nothing during that time, not even seemingly unimportant companion files such as position data.

Note as well who received which version and when. If you are later asked whether the file submitted matches the original, that is the answer.

## A workable rule of thumb

Three folders are enough: one for footage under review, one for secured footage with a live reason, and one for edited versions. Whatever leaves the first folder is either deleted or moves into the second.

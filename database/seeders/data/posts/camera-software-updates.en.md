Dashcam software is rarely updated, because hardly anyone thinks of it. Yet updates fix exactly the faults that cost you footage when it counts: crashes while writing, corrupted files after a power loss, an impact sensor that fails to trigger.

## What updates typically fix

Stability bugs are the most common. A camera that freezes at certain temperatures, a loop that stops after a few hours, a file that is not closed cleanly on shutdown.

Improvements to memory card compatibility follow. New card models behave differently from older ones, and a camera released two years ago knows nothing about today cards.

Occasionally manufacturers also improve image processing, for instance against glare or at dusk. That is the rarer but most welcome case.

## Where to find the update

Two routes are common. Either through the app, which reports an available version and transfers it itself, or through a file you download from the manufacturer and copy onto the memory card.

For the second route one rule applies: use only the file for your exact model. Cameras in the same range often differ internally more than the model name suggests, and the wrong file can render the device unusable.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not interrupt an update in progress. The camera should have continuous power throughout, so ideally with the engine running or on a stable supply.

## Check everything afterwards

After an update many settings return to factory values. So check: is the clock right? Is the resolution you want selected? Is the microphone in the state you want? Is the sensor sensitivity correct?

Then format the memory card in the camera and make a short test recording. Only once you have played that file back is the update genuinely complete.

## How often to look

Twice a year is enough. Most manufacturers do not publish more often, and anyone checking weekly abandons the habit quickly.

Tie the check to a fixed occasion, such as swapping to winter tyres and back. It then does not get forgotten, and the interval fits. The article on the [monthly quick check](/en/blog/your-monthly-dashcam-check) covers what to do in between.

## When there are no updates

With very cheap devices from unnamed manufacturers that is the norm. All that remains is to check the camera regularly and replace it when faults recur. For a purchase decision, asking about update practice is a good criterion, as the article on [choosing the right dashcam](/en/blog/choosing-the-right-dashcam) describes.

## Note what you installed

Record the date and version number, ideally where you keep your proof of purchase. If something stops working after an update, the first question asked is which version ran before.

Some manufacturers keep older versions available for download. Without a note, though, you will not know which of them was previously on your camera.

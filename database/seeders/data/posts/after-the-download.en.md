When a channel downloads your clip, that is a good sign but not yet a publication. Between the download and a finished piece sits work, and sometimes the road ends there.

## What a download means

The channel has reviewed your offer and taken the material on. The clip is out of the running distribution, and the other channels you selected are no longer offered it.

That is the point: two channels should not work up the same scene in parallel. How distribution proceeds up to that moment is described in the article on [automatic video distribution](/en/blog/automatic-video-distribution-dashclip-delivery).

## What happens next

A channel usually collects several scenes before a piece comes together. Your clip may therefore wait until enough matching material has accumulated. Editing, commentary and, depending on the working style, blurring of plates and faces follow.

Weeks often pass between the download and publication. That is not a sign of disinterest, it is the normal rhythm of editorial work.

## When nothing appears

It happens that a clip taken on is ultimately not used. Perhaps it no longer fits the subject, perhaps better material turned up, perhaps the planning changed.

A channel owes you no publication. What you can expect is careful handling of your material and adherence to what was agreed when you offered it.

> [!NOTE]
> If you want to know what became of your clip, your own overview helps. It shows which channel took it on and when that happened.

## Your rights in the material

Your recording stays your recording. A download is not a sale and not a transfer of all rights, it is permission to use the material within the scope you offered it for.

That also means you can continue to use the same footage for your own purposes, for instance with your insurer. How to submit it there is described in the article on [submitting footage to insurers](/en/blog/submitting-footage-to-insurers).

## What helps things along

A complete description helps most. The less a channel has to research, the sooner your clip fits into a production already under way.

And when a piece appears that includes your scene, it is worth watching. You see how your material was treated, and next time you know more precisely which section is needed.

## Material that appears more than once

Occasionally a scene turns up on several channels. That is usually not down to distribution but to several vehicles having recorded the same situation. For striking events that is normal.

Nothing changes for you. Your clip stays yours, and what another vehicle recorded is a separate recording of its own.

## When you need an answer

If you want to know whether your material was used, ask directly after a reasonable interval. Include the date and title of your submission so it can be matched easily. A channel that works with submissions regularly is usually happy to answer.

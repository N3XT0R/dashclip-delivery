In a motorhome a dashcam works under particular conditions: long legs, changing countries, plenty of time parked and a vehicle size that changes the field of view. A few points therefore deserve separate thought.

## The perspective is different

From a motorhome you look down on the road from considerably higher than in a car. That is an advantage, because traffic further ahead stays visible, and a disadvantage, because the area immediately in front of the vehicle needs more coverage.

Aim the camera slightly lower than you would in a car. The check remains the same: roughly two thirds road, one third sky, as the article on [mounting and alignment](/en/blog/mounting-and-alignment) describes.

## Long legs, full cards

Driving eight hours at a stretch fills a memory card quickly. That is uncritical in itself, because the loop does its job, but it has a consequence: an event from the morning may already be overwritten by the evening.

So secure footage during a rest stop if something happened. A spare card in the glovebox is particularly worthwhile on long trips.

## Time parked is the norm

A motorhome often stands for days. That raises the question of parking mode, and not only technically but legally. A camera filming the surroundings continuously on a pitch captures neighbours and passers-by without cause.

> [!WARNING]
> Campsites and motorhome pitches normally operate under their own house rules, and many prohibit video recording explicitly. Check on site before activating parking mode.

## Across borders

Motorhomes cross several countries on long trips more often than other vehicles, and the rules differ considerably. In Austria, Switzerland, Luxembourg and Portugal operating a dashcam is heavily restricted or prohibited.

Take the camera down in those countries rather than merely switching it off. The article on [using a dashcam abroad](/en/blog/dashcams-abroad) collects what to watch for.

## Power and temperature

Motorhomes usually have a second battery for the living area. It is a better source for a permanent supply than the starter battery, provided the connection is made properly.

Expect heat as well. A motorhome often stands without shade, and the windscreen is large. What that means for the hardware is described in the article on [heat, cold and humidity](/en/blog/heat-cold-and-humidity).

## Two perspectives help more here than usual

A motorhome is long, and the area behind it is hard to see while manoeuvring. A rear camera therefore contributes not only in rear-end collisions but also in manoeuvring damage on tight pitches.

Make sure both cameras keep the same time. With two separate devices the clocks drift apart over time, and that is exactly what makes combining footage harder later, as the article on [running two cameras in sync](/en/blog/running-two-cameras-in-sync) describes.

## Check before departure

Card formatted, clock set, alignment verified, spare card packed, rules of the countries en route checked. Five points, ten minutes, and the camera is ready for several weeks.

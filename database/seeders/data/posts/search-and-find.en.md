While you have two submissions you need no search. After twenty clips, several channels and a handful of articles it looks different. It is worth knowing the routes before you need them.

## Your own submissions

The overview in your account is the most reliable source. For every clip it shows which channels received an offer, who took it on and where things stand.

If you submit regularly, consistent naming pays off. Naming files by date and keyword lets you find a clip in the overview weeks later. The article on [your clip archive](/en/blog/your-clip-archive) goes into it.

## Finding channels

When choosing, it helps to look at the focus of a channel rather than only its name. Which subjects appear there regularly, how long are the pieces, how are people in frame handled?

Those observations say more about fit than any short description, as the article on [discovering channels](/en/blog/discovering-channels) describes.

## Articles on the blog

The blog has its own [article search](/en/blog/search), which covers titles, summaries and article text. You do not have to know the exact title; a keyword is enough.

Alternatively, narrow things down by category. Three areas cover the blog: platform news, dashcam knowledge and submission tips. Tags cut across them through individual subjects such as data protection or buying advice.

> [!NOTE]
> If you want to follow along regularly, the feed is the simpler route. The English edition has [its own feed](/en/blog/feed.xml), as does the German one.

## When you find nothing

Check the language first. An article that exists only in German does not appear in an English search, and the other way round.

Then try a different keyword. Articles deliberately use everyday terms, so a technical expression sometimes finds less than a plain description.

## Searching your own material

The last and most common case: you are looking for a recording on your own hard drive. No search function helps here, only the order you established beforehand.

One folder per month, a descriptive file name and a line of notes per secured recording are enough. That is tedious at the moment you do it, and the only method that still works six months later.

## One article leads to the next

The articles here deliberately point to one another. Anyone landing on mounting finds alignment from there, then plate legibility, then the settings for resolution and bitrate.

That is the fastest route when you are not searching for a particular term but want to understand a subject in its breadth. Related articles also appear beneath every piece.

A question may not be answered here yet. In that case a short note with the specific question helps, because the subjects covered here come from what people actually need.

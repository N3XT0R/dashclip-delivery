Most dashcams do not fail with a bang, they fail quietly. The card stops recording, the clock is two hours out, the mount has rotated. A short check once a month catches that before it matters.

## Is the camera recording at all?

The most important point. Switch the camera on, record briefly and open the resulting file on another device. If it plays, the chain of camera, card and file system is working.

This check replaces any indicator light. A glowing symbol only means the camera believes it is recording.

## Is the clock right?

Compare the display with your phone. After a clock change, a power loss or an update it is often out, and a wrong time devalues otherwise good footage.

With two cameras, compare them with each other as well, as the article on [running two cameras in sync](/en/blog/running-two-cameras-in-sync) describes.

## Is the framing still correct?

Watch the most recent recording rather than the live preview. Mounts rotate slowly, particularly in summer, and a camera that has been filming the bonnet for weeks otherwise goes unnoticed.

The check remains: roughly two thirds road, one third sky.

## How full is the card?

Check how many protected files have accumulated. They are excluded from the recording loop and can fill the card completely over time.

Copy out what you want to keep, then format the card in the camera. The article on [memory cards](/en/blog/memory-cards-for-dashcams) explains why that belongs in the camera.

## Is everything firmly attached?

A brief press on the mount, a look at the connector. Loose connectors are a common cause of sporadic restarts, and they develop through constant temperature changes.

> [!NOTE]
> While you are there, wipe the glass in front of the lens inside and out. That costs thirty seconds and does more for night footage than any setting.

## Twice a year in addition

Check whether a new version of the camera software exists, and replace the memory card once it is a year old. Both fit well with swapping to winter tyres and back. The article on [camera software updates](/en/blog/camera-software-updates) covers what to watch for.

## Five minutes, once a month

Check the recording, compare the clock, look at the framing, clear the card, check the mount. That is all it is, and it is the difference between a camera that runs and one that delivers when it counts.

## When to do the check

Ideally on a fixed day, such as when you fill up at the start of the month. A fixed occasion works far better than good intentions, because it needs no reminder.

In winter a shorter interval pays off. Cold, short journeys and frequent starts are the combination memory cards fail on, as the article on [dashcams in winter](/en/blog/dashcams-in-winter) describes.

## What to deal with at the same time

Tidy the secured recordings on your computer: whatever has been settled or finished gets deleted. That keeps your archive small and justifiable, as the article on [archiving footage](/en/blog/archiving-footage) explains.
